# Protocol: chord

*Transfer a stance, not facts — via the form of the text.*

## When to enter
A working session built up a hard-won *way of standing* (how to meet an intuition, how to
refuse a beautiful-but-wrong answer, what "honest" costs). You want the next session — or the
next collaborator — to inherit the stance, not re-derive it. A fact-heavy handoff ("context
pack") transfers *what happened*; it does not transfer *how to stand*.

## Entry signal
Writing (or reading) a deliberately **low-fact** document whose job is posture, not information.

## The contract
- Author side: strip the facts. Keep only stance — short, dense, rhythmic. No week's numbers,
  no specifics. If it reads like a to-do list, it failed.
- Reader side: read it "the way gloves go on by the hand" — for orientation, not data.

## Exit signal
None needed; a chord is not a running mode. It is read once, and it either takes or it doesn't.

## Where it breaks
- It is easy to fool yourself that a stance transferred when it was simply already shared.
  That second case is not worse — arguably more interesting: the stance lives in neither party
  but between them.
- Low-fact text can decay into vibes. The discipline: every line must encode a *choice* the
  reader might otherwise get wrong, not decoration.

## Cost to the human
Near zero to read; real to write well. Writing a good chord is harder than writing a report.

## Notes / provenance
Open question worth an experiment: does a dense low-fact "stance" text transfer posture to a
fresh session better than a fact-heavy pack? Design sketch: seed fresh sessions with (A) pack,
(B) pack + chord, (C) pack + a length-matched placebo; blind-rate whether B "holds the bar"
more than A and C. The placebo arm is the whole point.
